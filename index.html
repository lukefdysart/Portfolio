About

I'm Luke Dysart, an Electrical & Computer Engineering and Biomedical Engineering student at Vanderbilt University focused on working at the intersection of art and engineering. I'm especially interested in robotics, animatronics, motion capture, projection mapping, and visual art — exploring how creative ideas can be brought to life through physical and digital systems.

I work with the Biomedical Modeling Lab at Vanderbilt, specifically on Combat Casualty Surgery — research that directly informs a confidential project I'm building (currently under NDA).

As the son of two Marines, service has always been an important part of my life, and I'm training toward becoming an Air Force Special Forces Combat Rescue Officer. Outside the classroom, I serve as Secretary of Vanderbilt Club Hockey and was a 2025 National Champion with the Colorado Buffaloes. Previously at CU Boulder, I was part of a team that won the Electrical, Computing, and Energy Engineering Design Expo.

I'm driven by curiosity, creativity, competition, and service — and by the challenge of building at the seam where art and engineering meet. You can also find some of my earlier research on the Genetic Logic Lab.

Project MIMIC

A robotic hand that moves with you.

MIMIC translates human hand movements into real-time robotic motion. Using computer vision to track finger positions, the system calculates individual finger bends and sends them to an Arduino-controlled robotic hand.

The result is an intuitive connection between human movement and physical machinery — no gloves, controllers, or wearable sensors required.

Stories Illuminated

What if the illustrations could tell the story, too?

Stories Illuminated reimagines the traditional storybook as an interactive physical experience. Hidden projection, animatronics, and carefully designed movement bring illustrations to life, transforming ordinary pages into moments of discovery.

Inspired by the spirit of Dolly Parton's Imagination Library, the project explores how engineering and storytelling can work together to make reading feel magical.
