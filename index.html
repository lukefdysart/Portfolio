About

I'm Luke Dysart, an Electrical & Computer Engineering and Biomedical Engineering student at Vanderbilt University focused on working at the intersection of art and engineering. I'm especially interested in robotics, animatronics, motion capture, projection mapping, and visual art — exploring how creative ideas can be brought to life through physical and digital systems.

I work with the Biomedical Modeling Lab at Vanderbilt, specifically on Combat Casualty Surgery — research that directly informs my project Mary, an offline augmented-reality guide for medical procedures.

As the son of two Marines, service has always been an important part of my life, and I plan to serve in the military myself. Outside the classroom, I serve as Secretary of Vanderbilt Club Hockey and was a 2025 National Champion with the Colorado Buffaloes. Previously at CU Boulder, I was part of a team that won the Electrical, Computing, and Energy Engineering Design Expo.

I'm driven by curiosity, creativity, competition, and service — and by the challenge of turning imagination into something tangible. You can also find some of my earlier research on the Genetic Logic Lab.

Augmented Reality On-device AI Biomedical

Mary — Medical AR Interface

A self-explaining guide for any procedure, in augmented reality. Like the voice that walks a bystander through using an AED — but for every procedure and piece of equipment, hands-free, and able to see what you see. Mary recognizes the equipment, walks you through one calm step at a time, answers questions, and re-explains anything you miss — all running completely offline, on the device. Built alongside my work in Combat Casualty Surgery at Vanderbilt's Biomedical Modeling Lab.

Project MIMIC — Hand Mimicry Robotics

A robotic hand that mirrors your own in real time. A webcam tracks your hand with MediaPipe, custom joint-angle math estimates how bent each finger is (0–100%), and those values stream over serial to an Arduino driving servos. The live demo below runs the exact tracking pipeline right in your browser — no install, just your webcam.
